# Ghost-Prime: the 2-minute pitch

**Target:** 2:00 including the live demo, at an easy 140 words a minute.
**Rule:** every sentence below matches `showcase/FACTS.md` (checked 25 Sep 2026). Don't add claims. If
you change a line, check it against FACTS first.

**The one demo sentence (the "hero phrase").** It is the same, word for word, in this script, on the
pitch card, in `showcase/demo/DEMO.md` (Demo 5 and the cue card), on the poster, in the slides'
demo-slide notes and in the offline replay (Plan C). Don't change it on the day.

> **Go to the Wikipedia website, search for photosynthesis, and explain it to me in three simple sentences.**

The app's start screen has an example button that reads "Go to Wikipedia and explain photosynthesis
in three simple sentences". Those are different words, so don't click it during the pitch. Type or
say the hero phrase.

---

## The time budget

DEMO.md estimates **30–60 seconds** from sending the request to the answer starting. Reading the
answer aloud then takes about **30–40 seconds**. That second number is this script's own estimate:
the replay's scripted answer is about 90 words, and a live answer can be shorter or longer. Nobody
has timed either on the showcase setup yet. So you send it early, talk while it works, and go quiet
while it reads.

| Part | Clock | What you say | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook and send | 0:00 to about 0:20 | about 35 words, including the 17-word hero phrase if you speak it | about 20 s |
| 2. Talk over the wait | from about 0:20 until it starts reading | five lines in a fixed order, about 135 words (about 58 s). Both MUST lines are done by about 1:05. You stop the moment it reads. | 30–60 s |
| 3. The answer | starts between **0:50** (best) and **1:20** (worst) | nothing | 30–40 s |
| 4. After the answer | straight after it finishes | the rest of any MUST line you haven't said, then the short cost line if the stopwatch shows 1:45 or less | 0–25 s |
| 5. Close | last | 13 words | about 6 s |

- **Best case:** the answer starts at 0:50, usually while you are saying who it's for. It reads until
  about 1:20–1:30. Then you finish that line, say who built it and the short cost line, and close at
  about 1:45–1:55.
- **Worst case:** the answer starts at 1:20. You have said all five lines by then. It reads until
  about 1:50–2:00, and you close straight away, so the pitch ends at about 1:56–2:06. That can be a
  few seconds over. If your rehearsal looks like this, use the early send (see "If your rehearsal
  runs over 2:00").
- **Later than that:** the hard stop at **1:25** (part 2). Don't wait any longer.

Put a stopwatch where you can see it (your phone, face up on the table) and start it on your first
word. You need it for three moments: **1:10** (the first filler line), **1:25** (the hard stop) and
**1:45** (the last moment for the short cost line).

**How to read the cues**

- **[pause]**: stop for one breath and look at the judges.
- **[demo: …]**: do the action written next to it.
- **MUST**: never cut this line.
- **Filler**: a spare line. Say it only if you have finished the five lines and it still isn't
  reading.

## The script

### 1. Hook and send (0:00 to about 0:20)

Hi, I'm **[YOUR NAME]** from **[YOUR CLASS]** at **[SCHOOL]**. **[pause]**

What if you could use a computer just by talking?

**If you type** (the default for this long sentence in a loud hall). You typed the hero phrase into
the chat box before your slot (setup step 7), so it is already there.

> **[demo: point at the chat box]** I typed this before we started, so you don't wait. I could also
> say it. **[demo: press Enter]** (about 1 s, so it is sent at about 0:17)

**If you speak** (only if the mic got the phrase right when you tried it here at the venue). Allow
about 12 s for this: the phrase, two clicks and turning your speech into text.

> **[demo: click the mic button]** "Go to the Wikipedia website, search for photosynthesis, and
> explain it to me in three simple sentences." **[demo: click the mic button again to send]**

It may say a short line such as "Let me pull that up." That isn't the answer yet. Keep going.

### 2. Talk over the wait (from about 0:20)

Say these five lines in order. **The moment it starts reading its answer, stop talking, even in the
middle of a sentence.** Don't talk over it.

**2a. The problem**

Lots of people get stuck on computers. A chatbot tells you the steps, and you still do them yourself.
**[pause]**

**2b. The idea**

Ghost-Prime does the whole job. **[point at the browser]** It's using the browser by itself.
**[point at the step cards]** Every step shows up here.

**2c. Who it's for (MUST)**

It's for students who type slowly, people who find small text hard to read, people new to computers,
and busy teachers. I haven't tested it with these groups yet. That's my next step, and my ask.
**[pause]**

**2d. Who built it (MUST)**

I designed it, decided what it should do, tested it, and used an AI coding assistant (Claude Code)
to write much of the code.

> **Change this sentence** so it says exactly what **[YOUR NAME]** did. Use the same sentence in
> `qa.md` (the "What did YOU build?" answer) and on the pitch card. Don't make your part bigger or
> smaller than it was.

**2e. The cost line**

This is a normal school Chromebook. **[touch the Chromebook]** No pay-per-use bills. It runs on
Google's free AI plus a Claude subscription I already had, and it can run on the free tier alone,
though it's less capable at long tasks.

**The short cost line** (for after the answer, or when time is short): "No pay-per-use bills, just a
Claude subscription I already had."

**Fillers.** If you finish 2e and it still isn't reading, say these in order, and stop the moment it
reads. **At 1:10 on the stopwatch** (about 50 s after you sent it), say the first one even if you
haven't finished 2e: finish your sentence first.

1. "It reads the article first, so it can take up to a minute."
2. **[point at the step cards]** "It numbers every button and link on the page, then clicks by number."
3. "Chromebooks can already type what you say or read text aloud. This does the whole job."
4. "It also remembers you from day to day."
5. Point at the step card it is on and say what it shows, for example "Now it's opening the
   article." Only say what is really on the screen.

**Hard stop at 1:25.** If it still hasn't started reading, say:

> It's taking longer than usual here. I'll show you the answer at the booth.

Press the red **Stop** button (a square) next to **Send**. Presenter mode hides the Activity panel, so
use that one. Say the short cost line if you haven't said 2e, then close (part 5).

### 3. The answer (starts between 0:50 and 1:20)

Stop talking. Let it read all three sentences (about 30–40 s). You can point at the answer, but say
nothing until it finishes.

### 4. After the answer

1. If it cut you off during 2c or 2d, say the rest of that line. Say 2d if you hadn't reached it.
   Skip every other line you haven't reached, and all the fillers.
2. If you haven't said the cost line (2e) and the stopwatch shows **1:45 or less**, say the short
   cost line.
3. Close.

### 5. Close (13 words, about 6 s)

My ask: help me test it with real students and teachers. Thank you!

## If your rehearsal runs over 2:00

It runs over when the answer starts late or reads for a long time. Talking faster doesn't fix that.
Do these in order:

1. **Send earlier.** Pre-type the hero phrase and press Enter straight after your name line, before
   "What if you could use a computer just by talking?". Then say "I typed this before we started, so
   you don't wait. I could also say it." and the hook question while it works. This sends about 10 s
   earlier. Do it if, in your rehearsal, the answer took **more than 60 s to start** or read aloud for
   **more than 35 s**.
2. Say the short cost line in 2e instead of the full one.

**Never cut:** the hero phrase, the who-it's-for line with "I haven't tested it with these groups
yet. That's my next step, and my ask.", the who-built-it sentence, or the ask.

The demo stays the same in every case. If the site is slow or blocked, or there's no internet, use
the backups in the table further down. They are the same demo.

## Before your slot (10 minutes before)

1. Start the app with `bin/ghost-showcase --restart` (DEMO.md, section 2). Check that the line it
   prints says `browser=playwright`.
2. Check the badge says **AUTO** (the showcase launcher starts there, in big-text presenter mode).
   If not, type `/mode auto`. Never **FULL AUTO** in public. Not **PLAN** either: PLAN only looks and
   never clicks, so the demo would stop.
3. Type `/voice on`.
4. Type `/site strict`, then `/site allow wikipedia.org`, then `/site allow example.com`, then
   `/site list` to check. These are the same two sites as DEMO.md (example.com is the backup page).
5. **Warm up the browser.** Type `Open example.com and tell me what it says.` The first browser
   launch is the slow one (DEMO.md), so this gets it out of the way. **Check that the answer shows a
   CLAUDE tag.** Leave that browser window open, snapped to the right (**Alt + ]**), with Ghost-Prime on
   the left (**Alt + [**).
6. Press **Ctrl+N** for a fresh chat. (Presenter mode already hides the chat history.)
7. **Talk or type? Decide now.** In a loud hall, **type** this long sentence. Speak it only if the
   mic got the hero phrase right when you tried it here at the venue. (DEMO.md leaves this choice to
   this script.)
   - **Typing:** type the hero phrase into the chat box now, and **don't press Enter**.
   - **Speaking:** leave the chat box empty.
8. **Plan C:** if the preflight showed **more than 1.5 GB of free memory**, the replay tab is open
   (DEMO.md §2, step 8), with the hero phrase typed into its chat box and not sent. Otherwise don't
   open it now, because the Chromebook needs its memory for the AI and the browser. Starting it later
   takes about 20 s (`showcase/demo/replay.sh`).
9. **Stopwatch** ready, face up where you can see it.
10. Just before you start: the hero phrase is still in the chat box (if you type), and the badge still
    says AUTO.

## If the demo goes wrong (stay calm, never pretend)

| What happens | What you say and do |
|---|---|
| Not reading yet at 1:10 | "It reads the article first, so it can take up to a minute." Carry on with the fillers, and stop when it reads. |
| Still not reading at 1:25 (hard stop) | "It's taking longer than usual here. I'll show you the answer at the booth." Press the red **Stop** button next to Send, say the short cost line if you haven't said the cost line, and close. |
| The mic doesn't work, or the words come out wrong | "The mic isn't working here, so let me type it." Type the hero phrase and press Enter. Typing takes about 20–30 s, so after you send, say only 2c, 2d and the short cost line. The hard stop at 1:25 still applies. |
| Wikipedia is slow or blocked (the page never loads) | "Wikipedia is slow here, so let me use a tiny page instead." Press the red **Stop** button, type `Open example.com and tell me what it says.` and press Enter. This is DEMO.md's backup for Demo 5. |
| "Site not allowed" | You missed step 4 of the setup. Say "I've locked it to safe sites, and I missed one." Type `/site allow wikipedia.org` and ask again, or move on. |
| It clicks the wrong thing or gets stuck | "That's a real AI making a real mistake. That's why it shows every step." Press the red **Stop** button and move on. |
| No internet at all | "The internet here isn't cooperating, so here's a replay of the same demo. It's the real app's screen, but the answer is scripted, not live." Switch to the replay tab (if it isn't open, start it: about 20 s), make sure the hero phrase is typed there, and press Enter. The replay answers in about 15 s. |

**Never** say it worked if it didn't. Judges respect "it failed, and here's why" much more than a
cover-up.

## Lines you must NOT add (FACTS.md says they aren't true or aren't tested)

- "It controls my phone." The phone app is built but **not tested on a real phone**.
- "Just say 'hey Ghost'." The wake word is **not installed**. Use the mic button.
- "It always asks before doing anything." **Nothing asks "are you sure?" yet.** That's planned.
- "It's completely free" or "It works offline." Claude needs a subscription, and everything except
  the backup voice needs the internet.
- "Voice is fully tested." Say "Voice works in my own testing; the real mic and speaker are only
  checked by hand."
- "It switches to offline voice by itself." It is built to, but nobody has tried that for real
  (`qa.md`, the question about the free AI running out).
- "Extra usage is off" as a fact, until you have checked the setting and written it into FACTS.md.
- "Ghost-Prime is open source." Only say it's built with free, open-source tools (Electron,
  Playwright, SQLite, Whisper, three.js).
- "It has two AI brains" as the way it works today. The demo is all Claude. Use the CLAUDE-tag line
  above.
- "It's for young children" or "It's made for children." The AI services it uses are for people aged
  18 and over (`qa.md`, the question about children and under-18s).
- "It helps teachers" (or any other group) as if that's proven. Say "it's for", and "I haven't tested
  it with these groups yet."
- "It's the first" or "the only" assistant like this. Say what it does, and don't claim nobody else
  does it.
- "It works in Kinyarwanda." Not yet (see the Kinyarwanda question in `qa.md`).
- "It can control any app." It can't. It uses its own browser window, a terminal and your files.
- "Watch it read this picture." On the day it is pinned to Claude, and pictures on the Claude brain
  are only checked by hand. Don't offer a picture demo unless you rehearsed it on the Claude brain.
